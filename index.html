    Spotting the cameras

    • Flock Falcon (ALPR): small bullet camera on a pole, usually with a solar panel and a boxy housing. Photographs every passing plate.
    • Flock Raven: audio gunshot-detection sensor, often co-located with Falcons.
    • Flock Condor: pan-tilt-zoom live video camera for intersections and lots.
    • Other vendors (Motorola/Vigilant, Leonardo ELSAG) look similar; check DeFlock's identification guides before reporting.

    Contribute sightings

    • Report cameras on DeFlock; they flow into OpenStreetMap, which feeds this page.
    • Experienced mappers can tag nodes directly in OSM as surveillance:type=ALPR.
    • A wrong pin is worse than a missing one: only report cameras you have positively identified.

    Push back locally

    • File a public-records request with your police department for their Flock contract, camera locations, and search audit logs. DeFlock publishes request templates.
    • Check Have I Been Flocked to see if your plate appears in released audit logs.
    • Abuse reports are tracked by the Institute for Justice's ALPR abuse database; misused systems get contracts cancelled (23+ jurisdictions in 2026).

    Safety and scope

    • Flock-Off is for awareness and lawful civic action: mapping, routing, records requests, and public pressure.
    • Do not touch, disable, or vandalize cameras. That carries criminal liability and shifts the debate from privacy policy to property damage.
    • Detection here is passive and informational. It does not interfere with any system.
