How this tracker works

Evidence tiers

Cancelled, rejected, or expired claims are verified only with 3+ independent citations from verified sources. Everything else is marked pending validation*, with its exact citation count shown per record. * Fewer than 3 independent verified citations; shown while awaiting corroboration.

Source transparency

– verified citations and – unverified leads across the dataset. Every citation is classified by an explicit, version-controlled domain list (scripts/classify_sources.py); unknown domains fail closed to unverified. Advocacy organizations, social and video platforms, aggregators, and AI summaries count as leads, never as citations.

How cancellations happen

  • Contracts have renewal dates. A renewal vote is the highest-leverage moment: the agency must publicly defend the system, and the tracker above shows when yours comes up.
  • What has worked elsewhere: public-records requests for the contract and audit logs, packing the council meeting, and citing neighboring towns that already cancelled.
  • 23+ jurisdictions cancelled or rejected Flock systems in 2026; roughly 98 have done so since 2021 (DeFlock tally via press reporting). The playbook is proven.

Spotting the cameras

  • Flock Falcon (ALPR): small bullet camera on a pole, usually with a solar panel and a boxy housing. Photographs every passing plate.
  • Flock Raven: audio gunshot-detection sensor, often co-located with Falcons.
  • Flock Condor: pan-tilt-zoom live video camera for intersections and lots.

Contribute

  • Contract tip (new deal, renewal date, cancellation vote)? Open an issue with a source link.
  • Camera sightings go to DeFlock; they flow into OpenStreetMap, which feeds the map tab.
  • Check Have I Been Flocked to see if your plate appears in released audit logs.

Safety and scope

  • Flock-Off is for awareness and lawful civic action: mapping, records requests, public pressure, contract fights.
  • Do not touch, disable, or vandalize cameras. That carries criminal liability and shifts the debate from privacy policy to property damage.
