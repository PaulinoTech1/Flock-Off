Another Missouri Sheriff Cancels Flock Contract After Community Forum The Commerce Dispatch Aug 30, 2026 Share By Justin Stewart Burns Publisher, The Commerce Dispatch Pulaski County Sheriff Stacy Ball canceled his office’s contract with Flock Safety, ending the county’s use of automated license plate reader cameras after residents raised concerns about privacy and surveillance. Ball didn’t make the decision on his own, as some law enforcement agencies have done. Instead, he invited members of the community to a public forum and asked them to tell him what they thought about the program before he decided whether it should continue. Afterward, Ball said he took time to read through the comments from residents, research how similar programs have played out elsewhere, and wait for Flock Safety to respond to a list of questions he’d submitted to the company. Last week, he made his decision and he didn’t waver. The Pulaski County Sheriff’s Office will discontinue its use of Flock cameras and will not replace the system with a competing platform from Axon. “We are not going backward,” Ball wrote. “Drug dealers, don’t confuse protecting constitutional rights with taking our foot off the gas. We were coming after you before Flock, and we’ll be coming after you when it’s gone.” Ball made clear his decision wasn’t based on a belief that his deputies had misused the technology. He said deputies had used the cameras responsibly and for legitimate law enforcement purposes, and that he personally reviewed searches and audited the program himself. But after listening to residents, Ball ultimately decided that the concerns surrounding privacy and government surveillance outweighed the benefits of the system. “No piece of technology is more important than the trust of the people we serve,” Ball wrote. “You elected me to make difficult decisions. You also elected me to listen. I have listened.” Records show the county’s Flock contract runs through October 7, 2026. The Sheriff’s Office has already begun decommissioning the system throughout the city. Email correspondence between Ball’s office and Flock Safety, released as well, show the company acknowledged receipt of the cancellation request and offered to discuss options for keeping the technology in place before proceeding with the uninstall. Ball listened to his constituents and declined. Ball wasn’t responding to a lawsuit. No deputy had misused the system. He asked his community what they thought, and the concerns he heard were serious enough to act on. Before the forum even happened, Ball told residents he was looking forward to hearing from the people who actually showed up. “Until then, I look forward to speaking to the citizens from Pulaski County who show up.” They did and he acted accordingly. As The Commerce Dispatch has previously reported, Flock cameras have become a growing point of debate in Southeast Missouri and across the country. The technology can be an effective investigative tool for law enforcement, but questions about privacy, data retention, and access to an expanding network of license plate readers aren’t going away. The cameras in Pulaski County are already coming down. Share
