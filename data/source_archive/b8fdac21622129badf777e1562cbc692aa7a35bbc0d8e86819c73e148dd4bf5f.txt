GOVERNMENT Milton votes to end use of Flock surveillance cameras in town Jessica Trufant The Patriot Ledger Oct. 7, 2026, 12:15 p.m. ET The Milton Select Board has voted to end the town's contract with Flock Safety for use of its automatic license plate recognition system and remove about a dozen associated cameras around town. After hearing from residents regarding their concerns about Flock cameras at a meeting last month, the board, at its Oct. 6 meeting, voted to end the contract. Board members cited questions around surveillance, privacy and access to data, though member Meghan Haggerty stressed the concern lies with the technology company, not the Milton Police Department. On the South Shore, Duxbury, Marshfield, Scituate, Kingston, Rockland and Plymouth are without Flock cameras on public property. Many other communities do use them, though they are increasingly coming under scrutiny by residents. Massachusetts lawmakers are considering legislation that would implement guardrails by regulating what data the cameras could collect and who would have access to it. But at the moment, it is determined by the community using the technology. What are Flock cameras? Flock Safety automated license plate readers are AI-boosted cameras that capture the make, model, plate number, color and identifying marks of every vehicle that passes the field of vision of the device. That information is then stored in searchable systems. Police can then go into the database, search for the license plate of a suspect or missing person and find out if the license plate of the car they're driving has been picked up on any cameras. Many police departments share their databases with each other, making the system a glorified "be on the lookout" call-out. Law enforcement officials have praised Flock as a game changer for helping track down stolen vehicles, finding missing people and solving homicide cases. But privacy advocates say it's a violation to create searchable records of where all drivers have been. Some officers have even been reprimanded for using the technology to improperly track people, like former spouses. Reporter Jessica Trufant can be reached at jtrufant@patriotledger.com.
