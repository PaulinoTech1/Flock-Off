Photo: RailTypes , CC0 , via Wikimedia Commons NEW BEDFORD The New Bedford Police Department will not renew its contract with Flock Safety, the company behind the license plate reader cameras the city switched off in August, and the agreement will end when it expires Nov. 12, the department said Friday. It sent the company written notice Oct. 8. "Deputy Derek Belong sent written notice confirming the department would not renew the contract," police spokesperson Holly Huntoon wrote Friday in an email to The New Bedford Light. "That notice was being provided more than 30 days before the contract's expiration." The confirmation came a day after the City Council gave final passage to an ordinance barring the city from using license plate readers, according to the council president and the ordinance's sponsor. The same day, Mayor Jon Mitchell fired two police officers a department investigation found had misused the system, his office said. What the council passed The ordinance, on the council's Oct. 22 agenda for a second reading, says no city department "shall acquire, purchase, lease, license, renew, extend, expand, deploy, operate, or materially modify any ALPR system or Mass Location-Tracking Technology," and bars entering into, renewing or continuing contracts for them. City money cannot be used for them. Its definition of mass location-tracking technology leaves out ordinary fixed security video, body-worn cameras, in-car video and traffic-signal systems unless automated identification or persistent tracking is turned on. Councilor at Large Shane Burgo, who proposed the ordinance and chairs the Committee on Ordinances, said after the vote that he hopes the Mitchell administration signs it. "Doing so would demonstrate good faith in its stated interest in developing meaningful safeguards and regulations for surveillance technology," he said. "This debate has never been about opposing public safety. It is about recognizing that public safety and individual privacy are not mutually exclusive." The department's case for the cameras The department acquired the Flock system in 2024, before Police Chief Jason Thody took over. In a June question-and-answer page, the department said Flock had helped in homicide, robbery, shooting, stolen vehicle, break-in, vandalism and missing person investigations, and that in several cases its information "contributed to arrests, the recovery of stolen property, and the issuance of criminal warrants." The department said then that the cameras capture plates and vehicle details and do not use facial recognition, that data not saved for an investigation is deleted after 30 days, and that every search leaves an audit trail. It shared data with about 125 law enforcement agencies in Massachusetts and within 50 miles of the city, and said federal agencies, including U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, had no access. Thody suspended the system the week of Aug. 11 while he reviewed how it had been used. "Flock can be a valuable investigative tool, but we also recognize that technologies like this raise legitimate questions about privacy, access, information sharing, and oversight," he said at the time. Mitchell, speaking in September after a committee advanced the ban, said councilors had "rushed into things" and that with the technology disabled, "we can have a level-headed discussion about this stuff." What happens next Burgo said councilors will take up rules for license plate readers Monday, Oct. 26. He has described the ban as a temporary measure while officials work on more detailed rules, saying it does not say "never" to the technology but "not now." Thody, in a Sept. 24 letter, backed audits, training and limits on how long data is kept while asking that police keep control of day-to-day decisions such as where cameras go. The ordinance now goes to Mitchell, who can sign or veto it; under state law it takes effect if he does not return it within 10 days after it is presented to him. Without a renewal, the city's Flock agreement ends Nov. 12, and any new contract would be barred if the ordinance becomes law. Label: Reported by us, from the town's posted records.
