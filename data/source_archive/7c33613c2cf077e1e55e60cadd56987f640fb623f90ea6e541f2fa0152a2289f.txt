Home News Flash Automated License Plate Reader (Flock) Cameras in Provo City News Flash Automated License Plate Reader (Flock) Cameras in Provo City Transparency Portal - Featured Items Posted on February 03, 2026 | Last Updated on October 01, 2026 Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) Cameras in Provo City Provo City contracts with Flock Safety for Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) cameras. ALPRs, also called Flock cameras, are cameras that create a record of passing cars. They capture still images of the rear view of a vehicle traveling on a roadway at a set location. The images may identify the license plate, make, model, color and other identifying vehicle characteristics such as stickers and vehicle damage. Provo city ALPR cameras do not perform the following functions: noise detection, photography of persons, facial recognition, Bluetooth connections/personal devices, or continuous vehicle tracking. It is important to note that Flock Safety sells a wide variety of products and services. Though the terms “ALPR” and “Flock camera” are often used interchangeably, they are different: an ALPR is a type of camera that Flock Safety and other companies sell, while “Flock camera” can refer to a wider array of products offered by Flock Safety. Provo City primarily contracts with Flock Safety for ALPR cameras. Why does Provo City use ALPR cameras? The Provo City Police Department began using ALPR cameras in 2023 as an investigative tool to help solve crime. They are particularly effective in locating stolen vehicles, identifying vehicles associated with criminal activity, and assisting in investigations involving serious offenses such as assaults, abductions, and missing persons cases. The intention is to improve response times and provide officers with actionable information to better protect the community. How does the Flock ALPR system work? The Flock Safety ALPR system uses cameras mounted in fixed locations to capture images of the rear end of passing vehicles. The system may also record vehicle characteristics such as color, make, model, and unique characteristics like roof racks, vehicle damage, and bumper stickers. Once the information has been collected, the Flock system converts these license plate and vehicle images into a searchable database. This data is automatically deleted 30 days after collection. When officers are investigating a case that involves a vehicle, they can enter the license plate or other defining vehicle characteristics into the database search bar to look for matching vehicles that have been captured in the last 30 days. Provo City’s ALPR system also utilizes “hotlists,” or lists of vehicles associated with ongoing crimes and investigations. Examples of hotlist items include stolen vehicles or vehicles associated with active warrants. When a vehicle matches the hotlist, officers may be alerted so they can respond appropriately. Who has access to the ALPR database? Access to ALPR data is strictly controlled. Only authorized law enforcement personnel with a legitimate investigative purpose are permitted to access the system. Before officers may search the Flock database, they must use an individually assigned login to access the system. After logging in, officers are required to enter a relevant case number for their search. All searches of the database are logged. These searches, including the search terms, case number, and search frequency, are audited every 30 days to ensure compliance with department policy and applicable laws. The database is not available for public browsing or general surveillance or search purposes. Can Provo’s ALPR cameras track where people are coming from or going? No. These cameras capture a single image of a rear license plate at a fixed location and time. Depending on how many Provo City ALPR cameras a car passes, the database provides varying degrees of detail on a given vehicle’s whereabouts, but Provo’s network does not create a comprehensive record of movement within the city. As of August 11, 2026, the Provo Police Department utilizes a total of 24 ALPR cameras. These cameras cover only a small portion of Provo City’s roadways, which span over 640 miles.
