The Hamilton County Sheriff’s Office paused the internal use of Flock camera technology for personnel, according to an announcement made by Sheriff Charmaine McGuffey on Thursday, Sept. 3, 2026. The decision arrives as public scrutiny intensifies across Greater Cincinnati regarding automated license-plate readers and police surveillance. Hamilton County Sheriff Pauses Automated License-Plate Reader Deployment Sheriff McGuffey announced the temporary halt during a Thursday press conference called to address the agency’s deployment of Flock Safety cameras. According to a news release from the Hamilton County Sheriff’s Office, the briefing was designed to clarify how the agency uses the technology and outline what residents can expect moving forward. Operated by Flock Safety, the automated cameras capture photographs of vehicle license plates each time they pass into view, allowing law enforcement to track vehicle locations without securing a warrant. The pause by Hamilton County personnel places the agency among a growing number of local departments rethinking their surveillance capabilities. According to local reporting by Cincinnati.com, at least half a dozen communities in Greater Cincinnati—including Newport, Independence, Fort Mitchell, Erlanger, Fort Thomas, Edgewood, Pierce Township, and Oxford—ended their Flock camera programs over the summer due to mounting public anxiety over privacy and potential misuse. Four Northern Kentucky law enforcement agencies also discontinued their programs in recent weeks. Bipartisan Backlash and Legislative Pushback Against Surveillance Tech The expansion of automated plate-reading networks has triggered bipartisan criticism throughout the region. While law enforcement officials defend the tools as vital for solving violent crimes, civil liberties advocates argue that continuous data collection amounts to government tracking of everyday citizens. An Enquirer review of regional police records identified more than 1,100 license plate-reader cameras operating across Greater Cincinnati. Photo: wlwt.com Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky introduced legislation dubbed the “Flock-Off Act,” which aims to block federal funds from being used to purchase, install, operate, or upgrade automated license-plate readers and biometric surveillance systems. Representative Massie stated that taxpayers should not be forced to fund infrastructure that he believes infringes on individual privacy. Diverging Local Approaches to Transparency and Retention Police departments facing community pushback have responded with varying strategies. According to local records, Delhi Township police and Wyoming police established transparency websites detailing successful case studies, such as locating a missing elderly person and arresting a homicide suspect. Conversely, Butler County Sheriff Richard Jones defended his agency’s reliance on the technology, stating on Sept. 3 that he would not apologize for utilizing Flock cameras and emphasizing their ongoing value to criminal investigations.
